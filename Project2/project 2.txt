1. Domains in the Place of Residence

For this assessment, the residence is divided into the following security domains:

a) Perimeter/Outdoor Domain – compound, fence, gate, driveway, yard.
b) Building/Structural Domain – doors, windows, walls, roof, ceilings.
c) Electrical/Utility Domain – electricity supply, wiring, sockets, generator/inverter,    	gas/water systems.
d) Information Technology Domain – Wi-Fi router, phones, computers, CCTV and other connected devices.
e) Human/Occupant Domain – residents, visitors, domestic workers and other people with access.
f) Fire and Environmental Domain – cooking area, fire sources, drainage, weather exposure 	and emergency conditions.
g) Valuables/Asset Domain – money, documents, electronics, vehicles, appliances and other 	valuable property.

5 & 6. Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risk Categorization

Risk results when a threat can exploit a vulnerability and cause harm.

For this project, the categories are:

Critical: Could cause serious injury/death, major property loss, or significant compromise of important information.
Major: Could cause substantial damage, financial loss, disruption, or security compromise.
Minor: Limited impact that can generally be resolved without major consequences.

7. Mitigation Measures for Critical and Major Risks

A. Critical Risks

1. Burglary through weak doors/windows

Mitigation:

Install strong, good-quality locks.
Repair or replace damaged doors and windows.
Keep accessible windows secured when the residence is unattended.
Use appropriate exterior lighting.
Consider an alarm or other lawful security system.
Avoid leaving keys in obvious or accessible locations.

2. Residential fire

Mitigation:

Install appropriate smoke alarms.
Keep a suitable fire extinguisher accessible.
Maintain electrical appliances and wiring.
Keep flammable materials away from heat sources.
Establish a household emergency/evacuation plan.
Ensure occupants know how to contact emergency services.

3. Gas leakage/fire

Mitigation:

Have gas equipment inspected and maintained by qualified personnel.
Keep gas cylinders and equipment in appropriate, well-ventilated locations.
Check for signs of damaged hoses or connections.
Keep ignition sources away from suspected leaks.
Do not attempt unsafe repairs.
Ensure occupants know the appropriate emergency procedure.

4. Electrical fire or electric shock

Mitigation:

Have electrical installations inspected by a qualified electrician.
Avoid overloading sockets and extension cords.
Replace damaged cables and plugs.
Use appropriate circuit protection.
Keep electrical equipment away from water.
Disconnect faulty equipment and have it professionally repaired


B. Major Risks

1. Unauthorized access to personal devices

Mitigation:

Use strong, unique passwords.
Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
Keep phones and computers updated.
Lock devices when not in use.
Avoid sharing passwords unnecessarily.

2. Wi-Fi/network compromise

Mitigation:

Use a strong Wi-Fi password.
Change default router administrator credentials.
Keep router firmware updated.
Use modern Wi-Fi security such as WPA2/WPA3 where supported.
Create a separate guest network for visitors when available.

3. Theft of valuables

Mitigation:

Keep valuables out of public view.
Secure important items appropriately.
Maintain an inventory of valuable property.
Improve physical security around entrances and storage areas.

4. Theft or exposure of personal documents

Mitigation:

Store important documents in a secure location.
Avoid leaving sensitive documents in publicly accessible areas.
Destroy sensitive documents securely when they are no longer needed.
Keep digital copies of important documents securely backed up.

5. Water leakage/property damage

Mitigation:

Inspect pipes and plumbing periodically.
Repair leaks promptly.
Keep drainage systems clear.
Know where the main water shut-off point is located.
Avoid ignoring dampness or unexplained water accumulation.

6. Slips and falls

Mitigation:

Clean up spilled water immediately.
Repair damaged flooring.
Improve lighting in areas such as stairs and corridors.
Keep walkways free of unnecessary objects.
Use appropriate non-slip surfaces where needed.

7. Loss of important digital information

Mitigation:

Regularly back up important files.
Keep at least one backup separate from the primary device.
Use reputable security software where appropriate.
Keep operating systems and applications updated.
